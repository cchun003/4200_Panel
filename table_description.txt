# Table Descriptions and Captions

## Output Directory Tables

### table_1_tuition_didc.md
**Caption:** Table 1: Capitalization of Higher Education Tuition Subsidies (2012-2024)
**Description:** This table isolates the capitalization effect of the purely financial component of the higher education policy gap (in-state tuition). Models progress from raw Spatial OLS to full Spatial Difference-in-Discontinuities (DiDC) adding border-segment-by-year fixed effects and macroeconomic time-varying covariates (local property taxes, income taxes, real GDP. capita, and unemployment). Both single-way and two-way standard errors are mapped across baseline results.

### table_2_caliber_didc.md
**Caption:** Table 2: Capitalization of Elite Higher Education Institutional Caliber (Top 50, 2012-2024)
**Description:** This table isolates the capitalization effect of the institutional prestige gap, specifically defined as access to Top 50 elite public universities (Public Ivies). Similar to Table 1, the specifications transition from cross-sectional RD to the fully saturated panel DiDC. It highlights how the raw option value of prestige is priced into the housing market independent of financial aid.

### table_3_joint_didc.md
**Caption:** Table 3: Joint Capitalization Model (Tuition and Top 50 Caliber, 2012-2024)
**Description:** The baseline joint estimation. This model evaluates the "Tuition Cliff" by including both the financial subsidies (Tuition Gap) and the prestige option value (Top 50 Caliber Gap) simultaneously. 

### het_table_2_timeframes.md
**Caption:** Table 4 (Het): Timeframe Capitalization Heterogeneity (Pre-COVID vs. COVID-Era)
**Description:** This table explores sample stability and behavioral shifts across time. By splitting the sample pre-2019 and post-2020, it assesses whether the explosion of remote work and pandemic housing market distortions structurally altered the willingness of households to pay premiums for cross-border flagship university access.

### het_table_3_tax_cliff.md
**Caption:** Table 5 (Het): Marginal Tax Cliff Capitalization (Lowest Quartile Tax Differential)
**Description:** A test of Tiebout substitution limits. By restricting the sample solely to border dyads where the absolute state income tax difference is in the bottom 25th percentile, we equalize the primary fiscal driver of cross-border residential sorting. This isolates the willingness-to-pay for educational access in environments where the tax penalty for moving is negligible. 

### het_table_4_caliber_tiers.md
**Caption:** Table 6 (Het): Option Value Non-Linearities (Top 100 vs Top 50 Tiers)
**Description:** This table proves that higher-education capitalization is almost exclusively driven by elite institutions. It contrasts the baseline Top 100 Caliber measure against the Top 50 Caliber measure. Data reveals a massive tripling of the implied coefficient at the Top 50 border, showing that housing markets respond aggressively to elite public ivies but weakly to generic public state systems.

### het_table_5_pure_price.md
**Caption:** Table 7 (Het): Pure Price Elasticity (Constant Top 50 Caliber)
**Description:** This cuts the sample strictly to cross-border dyads where adjacent states provide identical access to Top 50 universities (Caliber Gap = 0). This completely neutralizes prestige option value from the model, directly estimating the isolated elasticity of the housing market strictly to the financial tuition cost of the borderline.
