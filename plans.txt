# Heterogeneity Analysis & Alternative Regression Plans

This document outlines the detailed execution logic for exploring heterogeneous effects and correcting our econometric models to find statistically significant and interpretable capitalization results.

## 1. Single-Way vs Two-Way Standard Error Clustering
**Status:** In Progress
- **Logic:** Two-way clustering (`~ zcta + border_dyad`) might be excessively punishing on the variance-covariance matrix. We will evaluate models with single-way clustering (`~ segment_id` or `~ zcta`) and present them alongside the original two-way clustered results for comparison.

## 2. Timeframe Sub-sampling (Pre-COVID vs COVID Era)
**Status:** In Progress
- **Logic:** The COVID-19 housing boom introduced intense structural breaks. We will slice the panel into a Pre-COVID sample (2012-2019) and a COVID-era sample (2020-2026) to test capitalization dynamically.

## 3. Alternative Measures of Y & Housing Architectural Controls
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Comparing prices per square foot assumes housing stock is identical. We need structural controls to prevent omitted variable bias.
- **Execution:** Update the Python `census` API querying script to add structural variables (`B25017_001E` Median Rooms, `B25035_001E` Median Year Built) and alternative dependent variables (`B25077_001E` Median Home Value, `B25064_001E` Median Gross Rent). 

## 4. Definitions of "Flagship" Caliber Tiers (Non-linear Option Value)
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Option value is likely non-linear. Households may not cross state lines for a Top 100 school, but access to a Top 20 public Ivy is a massive financial asset.
- **Execution:** Substitute the `delta_top_100_caliber` variable with Top 20, Top 50, and Top 150 variants. Observe if the coefficient ($\tau$) becomes positive and highly significant specifically at the most elite tiers.

## 5. The Marginal "Tax Cliff" Border
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Local property and state income taxes heavily dominate housing prices. At borders where tax regimes are virtually identical, the tuition gap becomes the primary financial differentiator.
- **Execution:** Compute the absolute state income tax differential ($|\Delta \text{Tax}_{st}|$) for each dyad. Restrict the estimation sample to the lowest quartile of tax differentials and re-run the DiDC model.

## 6. State Pairs with Similar Caliber (Pure Price Elasticity)
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Isolating pure price elasticity. If both states have the exact same number of elite institutions, the option value of prestige is equalized. Sorting is driven purely by tuition savings.
- **Execution:** Restrict the sample to dyads where `delta_top_50_caliber == 0` and evaluate the `delta_in_state_tuition` coefficient.

## 7. Interstate Tuition Reciprocity Agreements
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Crossing borders does not trigger out-of-state tuition if reciprocity agreements (e.g., WUE, MHEC) exist. The capitalization effect should be zero at these borders.
- **Execution:** Create a binary matrix flagging dyads with reciprocity agreements. Split the sample into reciprocity vs. non-reciprocity borders.

## 8. Spatial Distance to the Flagship Campus
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** The option value capitalization should be strongest in ZCTAs that actually lie within a reasonable driving distance of the flagship campus.
- **Execution:** Calculate distance from border ZCTAs to the nearest Top 50 university in their state via Geopandas. Split sample into "Close" vs. "Far".

## 9. Socioeconomic Segmentation (High vs. Low Income Neighborhoods)
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** High-income households are inelastic to tuition but sensitive to prestige. Middle/lower-income households are highly sensitive to the tuition gap.
- **Execution:** Subset local ZCTAs into income quartiles using `Median_Household_Income`. Run the tuition model on the bottom 50% and the caliber model on the top 50%.

## 10. Cross-Sectional Splitting (Urban vs. Rural)
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Housing supply elasticity drives capitalization. In rural areas, new housing absorbs the demand; in urban constrained locations, prices rise.
- **Execution:** Categorize ZCTAs into Urban Core vs Rural segments based on population density.
