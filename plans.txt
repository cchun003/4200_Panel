# Heterogeneity Analysis & Alternative Regression Plans

This document outlines the detailed execution logic for exploring heterogeneous effects and correcting our econometric models to find statistically significant and interpretable capitalization results.

## 1. Single-Way vs Two-Way Standard Error Clustering
**Status:** In Progress
- **Logic:** Two-way clustering (`~ zcta + border_dyad`) might be excessively punishing on the variance-covariance matrix, inflating our confidence intervals. 
- **Execution:** We will run the baseline Full DiDC model with single-way clustering (`~ segment_id` or `~ zcta`) and present it alongside the original two-way clustered results for comparison.

## 2. Timeframe Sub-sampling (Pre-COVID vs COVID Era)
**Status:** In Progress
- **Logic:** The COVID-19 housing market boom introduced intense structural breaks, fundamentally altering housing demand characteristics (e.g. rush for space rather than local amenities).
- **Execution:** We will slice the `estimation_didc_panel.csv` into a Pre-COVID sample (2012-2019) and a COVID-era sample (2020-2026), testing the capitalization of higher education amenities dynamically. If preferences for amenities shifted, significant results may emerge in the pre-crisis era.

## 3. Alternative Measures of Y & Housing Architectural Controls
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Comparing median prices per square foot across a border assumes housing stock is identical. We need structural controls to prevent omitted variable bias.
- **Execution (Data Pipeline Instructions):** 
    1. Open the original Python `census` API querying script.
    2. Add queries for structural variables from the ACS 5-Year Estimates profile: `B25017_001E` (Median Rooms), `B25035_001E` (Median Year Structure Built).
    3. We could also query `B25077_001E` (Median Home Value) or `B25064_001E` (Median Gross Rent) as alternative dependent variables.
    4. Re-run the data integration workflow (Tasks 1 & 2) to regenerate `estimation_didc_panel.csv`.
    5. Fit the models incorporating these new `X_ict` confounders.

## 4. Alternative Thresholds for Treatment Variable X
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Consumers may only value large threshold differences rather than reacting continuously per $1,000 of tuition gap.
- **Execution:** Discretize the `delta_in_state_tuition` gap into categorical bins or a binary indicator variable (e.g., $Gap > $5k). Use this indicator inside the DiDC interaction terms.

## 5. Cross-Sectional Splitting (Urban vs. Rural)
**Status:** Planned
- **Logic:** Housing supply elasticity matters. In rural locations where new housing is easily built, price capitalization fails to materialize.
- **Execution:** Categorize ZCTAs into Urban Core vs Rural segments based on population density, and estimate models separately on these distinct subsets.
