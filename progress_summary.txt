Project Progress Summary

1. Data Preparation:
The project has successfully constructed a 448k-observation longitudinal panel at the ZCTA level, synthesizing ACS housing data, tax simulations (via TAXSIM), BEA macroeconomic factors, and BLS unemployment rates. The final dataset is structured for a spatial Difference-in-Discontinuities (DiDC) design.

2. Econometric Estimations:
The script `task_3.1b_econometric_estimation.R` has been developed and executed. It employs the `fixest` package to run four incremental models for tuition and institutional caliber gaps:
- Naive OLS
- Spatial RD
- Base DiDC (with `segment_id^year` fixed effects)
- Full DiDC (adding time-varying tax and macroeconomic covariates)
Standard errors are two-way clustered by ZCTA and border dyad.

3. Current Results & Challenges:
As shown in `table_3_joint_didc.md`, the results are currently problematic for our hypothesis:
- While basic models (Naive OLS and Spatial RD) yield statistically significant estimates for the Tuition Gap LATE, introducing fixed effects and local controls (Full DiDC) eliminates the significance.
- In the Full DiDC joint model, the Tuition Gap LATE falls to -0.003 (p > 0.1) and the Caliber Gap LATE to 0.028 (p > 0.1).
- Strong covariates like the Effective Property Tax Rate, Log Real GDP Per Capita, and Local Unemployment absorb most of the variance (R2 leaps to 0.819).
- The two-way clustering of standard errors may also be overly conservative, widening confidence intervals.

In short, the baseline effects of higher education tuition and caliber subsidies on housing prices (measured as ln_median_ppsf) are completely washed out by local economic structures and fixed effects. This necessitates further heterogeneity analysis and alternative model specifications to uncover underlying mechanisms.
